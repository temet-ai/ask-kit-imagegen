# Checks for the requested asset

Use the relevant section, without adding deliverables or imposing a house aesthetic.

## Photos and products
Specify viewpoint, framing, useful material texture and plausible lighting. Preserve product
geometry, labels and identity from references. Check hands, repeated parts, reflections,
contact shadows and whether the image looks appropriate at its intended display size.

## Logos, icons and cutouts
Check the silhouette at small size, internal negative space, alignment, clipping and fine edges.
Use the existing identity when supplied. Exploration means different ideas when requested;
do not generate extra variants by default. A generated raster is not an editable vector logo.
Request real transparency through the built-in tool; a drawn checkerboard is a failed cutout.

## Website and mobile mockups
Use the caller's real content, viewport, density and project assets. Make the important task and
controls legible. Check text hierarchy, spacing, clipping and practical layout. Avoid filling
the screen with fake statistics, decorative badges or invented marketing copy. Do not force a
page into a fixed number of sections or extra images. Mockup pixels do not implement a website.

## Posters, text, diagrams and educational images
Group the exact supplied text into a readable hierarchy, with sufficient room for labels.
Check every word, punctuation, arrow and association. Compare numbers against the supplied data.
For a chart or screen, convey state through labels or shape as well as colour. Follow any
caller accessibility requirements. If exact plots, editable text or vectors are essential,
tell the caller which part needs their code-native workflow; don't silently replace the image.

## Sprites, illustrations and sets
Maintain character proportions, palette, line weight, lighting and camera across related jobs.
Use an approved earlier asset as an explicit reference. For sprites check full silhouette,
consistent scale and padding. Generate a sprite sheet only when requested; check grid, cell
count and clipping. Repeat the chosen visual constraints for every asset in a requested set.

## Edits and compositing
Compare input and output. Inspect the requested change and the regions that should be unchanged:
identity, pose, product details, framing, lighting, background and exact text as applicable.
Match scale, perspective, light direction and edge quality when inserting material. An unrelated
image that happens to contain the requested subject does not pass an edit check.
